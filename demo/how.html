How the twin works

The twin is a digital copy of the weather of India. It uses the weather of 3 days. It calculates the weather for the next 10 days, one day at a time.

What the twin calculates

The twin calculates 6 values for each point of a grid of 32 × 36 points. The values are temperature, rain, 2 wind values, sea-level pressure and water vapour.

Each grid point is 1 degree wide. That is approximately 110 km.

How the network works

The twin is a small neural network. It calculates the change of the weather for 1 day. Then it uses its own result to calculate the next day.

Each day, the network looks at 6 grid points around each point. Thus, a change moves at most 6 points in 1 day. A change cannot jump across India.

The 3 pages

Forecast shows how good the twin is. It starts from 3 real days and calculates the next 10 days. Then it compares them with the real weather.

Climate twin shows what-if universes. It starts from the same 3 real days twice: universe A as it was, and universe B with a change to the last day, for example a Bay of Bengal 3 °C warmer.

Flood risk changes the forecast of the twin into flood alerts for the states, and into the number of people who live in the alert area.

How to use the Climate twin page

  1. Select a starting state: 1 of 9 real 3-day periods.
  2. Select a change to the start: 1 of the shocks of the organisers, or "Your own change".
  3. For your own change, select where, what and how much. "Where I click on the map" puts an 8° × 8° box where you click. A change of 0 must change nothing, and the twin agrees.
  4. Select a field and a view: Two universes side by side, or the Difference (red: more, blue: less).
  5. Click the arrow buttons, or push the ← and → keys, to go 1 day back or forward. The panel tells the largest change, the values at that point in the 2 universes, and how far the effect has moved from the box.

What the Forecast page shows

Compare shows 2 maps side by side: the real weather on the left and the twin forecast on the right. The 2 maps use the same colours and move together. The panel below the maps shows the skill of each day. The skill uses the method of the organisers' kit: 1 minus the RMSE of the twin, divided by the RMSE of the better simple forecast ("repeat yesterday" or the long-term average). Above 0, the twin is better than both. Forecast shows the twin forecast only.

Move the pointer over a map to see the state or union territory and the values at that point.

Flood risk

Open the Flood risk page. The flood-risk module adds the rain of each day and the 2 days before it. It compares this 3-day rain with the 3-day rain that is rare for that place and month, from 2005 to 2021. There are 3 alert levels: watch (rarer than 97% of 3-day periods, and more than 40 mm), warning (99%, and more than 70 mm) and severe (99.7%, and more than 100 mm).

The twin forecast becomes weaker at its peaks after some days. Thus, the twin uses lower limits on later days. The limits make a twin alert as rare as a real alert.

A state is under an alert when at least 10% of its area has that level. The page names these states and tells how many days ahead the alert is. Very small union territories are not named, because the grid is too coarse for them.

The page also shows how many people live in the alert area. The population comes from WorldPop 2011 (CC BY 4.0). It is a fixed map from 2011, thus the number of people today is larger.

The page is the twin only. Two copies of the weather evolve one day at a time from the same start: "Before" without a change, "After" with the changes you apply at any day. Press → to calculate the next day. Each day shows the people and the states at flood risk, before and after. 4 windows show past floods.

Limits: the module uses rain only. It does not know rivers, dams, drainage or where people live. Each grid point is approximately 110 km wide, thus a flood in one city can be too small to see.

Where the data comes from

The twin runs on this computer. The page sends each request to the twin server. The page does not show saved results. The map does not use the internet.

The borders of India, its states and union territories come from the DataMeet India community (CC BY 4.0). They show India as the Survey of India shows it.
